Page 03 • My Work

What I Have Built

Three real projects from my student journey. Each one taught me something different about design, computing, teamwork, and improvement.

01 • FIRST DESIGN PROJECT

Nepal Valentine's Day Infographic

I created this infographic during Valentine's Day for our Empowerment Technology (E-Tech) subject. Our teacher asked us to choose a country and present information about its Valentine's Day culture, so I chose Nepal and designed the infographic myself.

The project became more meaningful because our teacher held a design competition. The first-place winner received ₱1,000, while second place received ₱500. I earned second place.

What I learned: My teacher said the design was clean and very good, but I did not maximize the available space because I used a large heart as a major design element. That feedback taught me that good design is not only about making something attractive; it is also about using space wisely. I know I can improve this in my next projects.

My role: Research, layout, and complete visual design.

Tools: Digital infographic/design tools.

02 • COLLEGE PROJECT

Computing Through Time

This was one of my first-year Introduction to Computing projects. It focused on the Pascaline and how early computing devices helped solve problems that people once handled manually.

I enjoyed this project because it connected an older computing device with the technology we use today. It helped me understand that modern computing developed through many earlier ideas and inventions.

What I learned: Computing did not become advanced overnight. Learning about earlier devices helped me appreciate the process of improvement that led to modern technology.

My role: Student researcher and project designer.

Tools: Digital infographic/design tools.

03 • COLLEGE GROUP ACTIVITY

Hospital Patient Management System

This was a group activity for my first-year Introduction to Computing subject. Our topic was a Hospital Patient Management System, showing hardware, software, data, people, procedures, the input-process-output cycle, and possible system problems.

Although it was a group activity, I took responsibility for the overall visual design. I asked my group members to research information that we could use, while I organized that information and designed the project in my own way.

What I learned: This project taught me that design and teamwork can work together. Research gives a project its information, while good organization makes that information easier to understand.

My role: Main designer and visual organizer; coordinated the information provided by group members.

Tools: Digital infographic/design tools.
